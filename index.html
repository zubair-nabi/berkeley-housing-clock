Berkeley approves new housing in about four months. Then it waits a year or more for anything to be built.

The public argument about Berkeley housing assumes the city is slow to approve. The record says approval is the fast part.

Everything on this page is fetched live from the State of California and the City of Berkeley when you load it. No estimates, no modelling.

09

Where this comes from

Six sources. Most are queried in your browser as the page loads. Two are not, for reasons given below, and both say so.

HCD Annual Progress Reports

Berkeley's filings to the State: applications, approvals, permits, completions, affordability. Every figure on this page except section 03 comes from here. Fetched live from the California Open Data Portal.

-

Berkeley GIS: addresses and parcels

62,000 address points carrying parcel numbers, plus lot and building size. This is what the address lookup queries, live from the City's ArcGIS server.

-

ZAB agendas

What the Zoning Adjustments Board hears next. Published as PDFs from a server that sends no CORS header, so a browser cannot read them: they are parsed in this repository twice a week instead. The parser is here.

-

CEQAnet

Environmental filings for both the City and UC Berkeley, in the State clearinghouse within days. It sends an open CORS header, so unlike the agendas this one is read live in your browser.

-

Housing Element sites

The 382 parcels Berkeley identified in 2023 as able to accommodate its state allocation. A cycle snapshot rather than a live layer, which is what a housing element inventory is meant to be.

Adopted 2023

Parcel geometry and BART

Parcel outlines, the sites inventory and the Richmond line alignment, cached in geo/. Berkeley's GIS server rate limits hard, so re-querying it on every visit would get the page blocked. BART's alignment comes from the agency's published GTFS feed.

What this cannot tell you.
A project shown as "waiting" may hold a permit the City never reported to the State, the state file is known to drop real filings. Every number on this page other than section 03 stops at December 2025, because the State publishes a year in arrears and 2026 will not appear until April 2027. Section 03 is the exception and is drawn from agendas and environmental filings, which are notices of intent rather than counts of homes. UC Berkeley's roughly 2,150 student beds are exempt from city permitting, so they appear in the clearinghouse but in none of the totals.

We can show you that a project is stalled. We usually cannot show you why. Financing, rates and construction costs are in no public dataset. If you know why one of these is stuck, that is the most useful thing you can tell us.
